The Course Gonzales → Houston · 188.81 mi · 36 legs
Why This Race Matters 190 years of history underfoot
🔫
Come and Take It — Gonzales, 1835
On October 1, 1835, Texian settlers dared the Mexican Army to take their cannon. They fired it instead — the first shot of the Texas Revolution. Every TIR team starts with a cannon blast from this exact spot. When you crossed that start line, you crossed it too.
🏃
The Runaway Scrape
After the fall of the Alamo in 1836, General Sam Houston ordered a strategic retreat eastward. Settlers fled across the same roads your team just ran — in mud, panic, and rain. The TIR route follows that exact path from Gonzales to Houston.
⚔️
San Jacinto — April 21, 1836
After weeks of retreat, Houston's army turned and attacked. In just 18 minutes, they routed Santa Anna's forces and won Texas independence. The battlefield stands just miles from where your team finished.
🏅
The Race Since 2008
Founded by Jay and Joy Hilscher, TIR debuted in 2008 as Texas's answer to Hood to Coast. Now 150+ teams and 3,000+ runners make the journey each March. The course record stands at 5:40/mi.

The Story
Start: 11:09 AM · 76°F · Clear Finish: 11:21 AM Mar 29 · 82°F Night legs: 16–28 (50–64°F) Peak heat: legs 1–10 (~75–78°F) · Finish: 78°F Fastest leg: JP · L27 · 6:19 Hardest draw: Scott Cooke · EDS 85

Twelve runners. Two vans. One cannon blast in Gonzales. Mark Verville stepped out first at 11:09 AM under a clear Texas sky, setting Van 1 in motion through the rolling Hill Country. By leg 3, JP Giner Martinez dropped a 6:32 — a signal to anyone watching that this team meant business. The afternoon brought climbing heat toward 78°F, punishing everyone who drew the early rotation. Van 2 picked up the baton mid-afternoon with Sherif Mabrouk opening at 6:50, immediately setting a pace the whole van would try to match. When darkness fell east of the Colorado River, Van 1 answered with its defining moment: Glen Lubbert ran 6.77 miles on the 4th-hardest leg in the dark at 9:22 PM in 6:46 — one of the best single performances of the entire race. Andrew Wildman followed with a 6:55 on the 5th-hardest leg at 10:08 PM in 62°F. The overnight shift was a masterclass. By at 4:02 AM, JP came back with a team-best 6:19 before the sun came up, while Sherif delivered a 6:41 at 2:09 AM — ice water in the veins. As Houston's Sunday morning heat climbed quickly toward its afternoon peak, the final rotation faced the hardest legs of the course. Scott Cooke drew the single hardest leg on the entire course — not a bad assignment after two prior hard efforts. Lance Pyburn carried the team's final baton leg across the finish at Saint Arnold Brewery at 11:21 AM. 188.81 miles of Texas history, 24 hours and 12 minutes. 32nd overall.
