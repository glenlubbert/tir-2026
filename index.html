What the conditions
actually cost you


Raw pace is your Apple Watch moving pace — what you actually ran. Adjusted pace answers a different question: if these same legs were flat, 67°F, and medium difficulty, how fast would that same effort have produced? It divides your actual pace by the condition factor — so harder conditions produce a faster adjusted pace, reflecting that your effort was worth more than the raw number suggests.

Why this race matters

190 years of
history underfoot


1835
Come and Take It

On October 1, 1835, Texian settlers in Gonzales dared the Mexican Army to take their cannon — and fired it instead. The first shot of the Texas Revolution came from the exact town where your race began. Every TIR start gun echoes that moment.

1836
The Runaway Scrape

After the Alamo fell, Sam Houston ordered a retreat east. Settlers fled in panic across the same roads you just ran — through Gonzales, past Shiner, through Columbus, toward Houston. The TIR route follows that path exactly.

18 min
Battle of San Jacinto

April 21, 1836. Houston's army attacked Santa Anna's forces near where you finished. Texas won independence in 18 minutes. The battlefield sits just miles from Saint Arnold Brewery. So does your finish line.

2008
The Race Begins

Founded by Jay and Joy Hilscher, TIR debuted in 2008 as Texas's answer to Hood to Coast. Now 150+ teams and 3,000+ runners make the journey each March. The course record stands at 5:40/mi. Your team ran 7:41.
